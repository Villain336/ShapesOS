# Advertising on Pinterest: A Playbook from Pinterest's Official Advertiser Guidance

*Original synthesis of knowledge learned from Pinterest Academy advertiser guidance. Ideas are expressed in Marketr's own words; factual attribution to Pinterest's official curriculum/guidance.*

## 1. The core insight: discovery happens before intent

Pinterest is not a search engine and not a social feed — it is a planning surface. People arrive weeks or months before they buy, collecting ideas for kitchens, weddings, wardrobes, trips, and projects that do not exist yet. This single fact reshapes every decision in this playbook:

- **Your content must arrive early in the decision window.** By the time the buyer is comparing prices on a retailer site, the Pinterest battle is over. Show up when the dream is forming.
- **You are competing with ideas, not with ads.** A pin that sparks "I could do that" beats a pin that shouts "buy now."
- **Plan for the calendar people actually live on.** Holiday planning, wedding season, back-to-school, home refresh — these cycles start months before the event. If your seasonal creative launches in December, you missed Christmas.

## 2. Creative craft: the anatomy of a strong pin

Pinterest's official creative guidance converges on a clear formula. Treat this as a checklist for every promoted pin:

1. **Vertical, 2:3 ratio.** Vertical pins dominate the feed layout. Square or horizontal creative is served at a disadvantage — reformat it or do not run it.
2. **Show the product clearly.** Lifestyle context is good, but the product must be unmistakable. If a viewer cannot tell what you sell within two seconds, the pin fails.
3. **Text overlay.** A short, legible headline burned into the image dramatically lifts comprehension. Keep it under a handful of words, large type, high contrast.
4. **Logo present but subtle.** Brand presence matters for attribution and trust, but the logo should support the pin, not star in it. A small corner placement is the standard.
5. **Compelling titles and descriptions.** The title is what the user reads; the description is what search reads. Write titles for humans (benefit-led, specific) and descriptions with the natural keywords a planner would type.
6. **Video: 6–15 seconds, designed for sound off.** Most Pinterest video is watched silently. Open with a strong visual hook in the first two seconds, carry the story with motion and text overlays, and treat any audio as a bonus rather than a requirement.

## 3. Targeting: keywords plus interests, separated for clean delivery

Pinterest targeting has two primary levers:

- **Keywords** — the terms people type when searching and the concepts Pinterest associates with boards and pins. Category keywords (e.g., "small kitchen ideas") capture high-intent planners.
- **Interests** — broader behavioral groupings for reaching people in a planning mindset even when they are not searching your exact term.

The structural discipline the guidance emphasizes: **separate ad groups so each audience gets its own delivery.** Do not bundle keyword targeting and broad interest targeting in the same ad group — the auction will favor one and starve the other, and you will never learn which audience actually performs. One targeting strategy per ad group, with enough budget for each to deliver.

## 4. Full-funnel structure — run all layers simultaneously

Because the planning window is long, Pinterest rewards advertisers who cover the whole funnel at once rather than sequencing it:

- **Awareness: broad lifestyle creative.** Wide interest targeting, inspirational imagery, no hard sell. Goal: get on boards early. Success metric: saves and close-ups.
- **Consideration: shopping and catalog pins at category keywords.** This is where product-forward creative lives — clear product, price visible, linking to a shoppable page. Target the category keywords where planners are actively comparing options.
- **Conversion: retargeting.** Re-engage site visitors and people who saved your pins. Saves are uniquely valuable on Pinterest because they persist — a saved pin keeps working for months, and the saver has declared future intent.

The practical setup: three campaign groups (or clearly separated ad groups) running in parallel, with creative and targeting tuned to each layer's job. Do not try to make one pin do all three jobs.

## 5. Seasonal calendar: plan months ahead

Pinterest's guidance treats the seasonal calendar as a core planning tool, not a nice-to-have:

- Map your category's planning peaks and work backward. Home decor planning for December starts in late summer; wedding content peaks in winter for spring weddings.
- **Launch seasonal creative 6–10 weeks before the event**, when planners are actively saving.
- Keep evergreen winners running underneath the seasonal layer so the account never goes dark.
- After each season, review which pins earned the most saves — saves are the leading indicator of next season's winners.

## 6. Catalog and shopping ads: accuracy is the product

For catalog-driven campaigns, the feed is the creative. The guidance is strict on hygiene:

- **Pricing must be accurate and current.** Mismatched prices between the pin and the landing page kill trust and waste spend.
- **Product titles and images in the feed should match the creative standards above** — clear product depiction, good imagery, complete attributes.
- Refresh the feed on a fixed schedule; stale availability data (out-of-stock items still promoted) is one of the fastest ways to burn budget and credibility.
- Use catalog sales campaigns for the consideration layer and retargeting for the conversion layer.

## 7. Measurement: tag, then look beyond the click

Install the Pinterest tag across the site and define the full event set — page visits, add to cart, checkout, signup. Then measure like a planner's platform, not a last-click platform:

- **New-to-brand metrics** deserve a permanent place in your reporting. Pinterest routinely introduces buyers who have never purchased from you; last-click attribution undervalues this.
- Track **saves and board adds** as leading indicators. A spike in saves today is revenue in eight weeks.
- Use conversion lift or holdout studies when budget allows, because the long decision window makes standard attribution windows misleading.

## 8. Creative refresh cadence

Pinterest creative decays more slowly than feed-based platforms (pins have a long half-life), but decay still happens:

- Review creative performance monthly; retire pins whose engagement has flattened for several weeks.
- Refresh winners with variations rather than replacements — same product, new angle, new text overlay, new lifestyle context.
- Feed new creative into the awareness layer first; proven winners graduate to consideration and conversion layers.

## 9. The launch checklist

1. Pinterest tag installed with full event coverage (verify firing on key pages).
2. Creative built to the 2:3 checklist: clear product, text overlay, subtle logo, strong title/description.
3. Video assets cut to 6–15 seconds with sound-off storytelling.
4. Ad groups separated by targeting strategy (keywords vs. interests), each funded to deliver.
5. Three funnel layers live: awareness (lifestyle), consideration (shopping/catalog at category keywords), conversion (retarget site visitors + savers).
6. Seasonal calendar mapped with creative launching 6–10 weeks ahead of peaks.
7. Catalog feed validated: accurate pricing, availability, and imagery.
8. Reporting includes saves, new-to-brand, and a longer attribution view than default.
