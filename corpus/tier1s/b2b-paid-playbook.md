# B2B Paid Advertising Playbook

*Original synthesis of knowledge learned from LinkedIn Marketing Labs. Marketr-original words; factual attribution to LinkedIn's official curriculum.*

## Why B2B paid is a different sport

B2B advertising buys attention from buying committees, not individuals. Clicks are expensive (often $8–$20+ on LinkedIn), sales cycles run months, and the person who clicks is rarely the person who signs. The playbook below is built for that reality: precision over volume, proof over promises, and measurement that survives the long cycle.

## Targeting: the professional graph is the product

LinkedIn's targeting is its moat — job title, function, seniority, company size, industry, skills, and company lists. Operating rules:

1. **Target the buying committee, not the persona.** For a mid-market SaaS sale that's typically 3–6 roles: the end user, the manager, the economic buyer, IT/security. Build one audience per role and speak to each role's pain — the CFO and the practitioner do not care about the same slide.
2. **Matched Audiences for precision.** Upload target account lists (ABM), retarget website visitors, and build lookalikes off your best customers — not off all visitors. A lookalike of "everyone who ever visited" is a lookalike of nobody.
3. **Minimum audience sizes are real.** Below ~50,000 for most objectives the delivery system can't optimize; below ~300 for matched-audience campaigns you get delivery warnings. If your target account list is 200 companies, broaden with firmographic targeting rather than running a starved campaign.
4. **Exclude ruthlessly.** Current customers, competitors, job seekers (exclude job-title "seeking" patterns via seniority/function filters), and existing pipeline — every irrelevant click at B2B CPCs is a steak dinner wasted.

## Creative that survives the committee

- **Lead with the outcome, not the product.** "Cut month-end close from 9 days to 3" beats "AI-powered finance automation platform" every time. B2B buyers are risk-averse; specificity is credibility.
- **Social proof is the creative.** Customer logos, named results, peer quotes, and third-party badges (G2, analyst mentions) do more work than any headline formula. One strong proof point per ad.
- **Documents and thought leadership outperform product pitches** for cold audiences. A genuinely useful framework, benchmark report, or teardown earns the click that a demo request never would — then retarget the engagers with the product story.
- **Video for the story, single-image for the offer.** Short video (30–60s) for problem/agitation and customer stories; crisp single-image or carousel for the concrete offer and CTA.

## Lead Gen Forms vs. landing pages

Native lead-gen forms convert at dramatically higher rates than landing pages (no load time, pre-filled professional data) — but lead quality is lower. The doctrine:

- **Use forms for top-of-funnel** (content downloads, event registrations, newsletter) where volume matters and intent is light.
- **Use landing pages for bottom-of-funnel** (demo requests, pricing) where friction filters tire-kickers.
- **Always sync to the CRM immediately** and score fast. A B2B lead contacted in 5 minutes converts at multiples of one contacted in 5 hours. Form volume without speed-to-lead is just an expensive email list.

## Full-funnel structure

1. **Awareness (cold):** thought-leadership content to the buying committee at target accounts. Objective: video views / engagement. Measure: reach in target accounts, content engagement rate.
2. **Consideration (warm):** product stories, comparisons, customer proof to engagers and site visitors. Objective: website visits / lead gen. Measure: marketing-qualified leads, cost per engaged account.
3. **Conversion (hot):** demo/trial offers to high-intent retargeting pools. Objective: conversions. Measure: sales-qualified leads, pipeline created, cost per SQL.

Never run only the bottom layer. A B2B account with no awareness layer is paying premium CPCs to harvest demand that doesn't exist.

## Measurement across the long cycle

- **Optimize to pipeline, not leads.** Connect ad platforms to the CRM and import offline conversions (SQL, opportunity, closed-won). Optimizing to form-fills trains the system to find form-fillers; optimizing to revenue trains it to find customers.
- **Account-level reporting.** In ABM, the unit of success is the account: how many target accounts engaged, how many entered pipeline, what the blended cost per engaged account is. Campaign-level CPL lies; account-level pipeline tells the truth.
- **Attribution sanity:** B2B touches are too many for last-click. Use multi-touch or at minimum position-aware reporting, and never kill an awareness campaign based on its direct-attributed pipeline.
