# Advertising on Snapchat: A Playbook from Snapchat's Official Business Guidance

*Original synthesis of knowledge learned from Snapchat for Business guidance. Ideas are expressed in Marketr's own words; factual attribution to Snapchat's official curriculum/guidance.*

## 1. The non-negotiable: full-screen 9:16 vertical video

Snapchat is a full-screen vertical medium, and the guidance treats anything else as a defect. The rules:

- **Shoot vertically, not cropped.** Creative conceived for horizontal and cropped to fit reads instantly as an outsider. Storyboard for 9:16 from the first frame.
- **Fill the whole canvas.** Letterboxing or pillarboxing breaks immersion and signals "this ad is from somewhere else." Design edge to edge.
- **Design for the first two seconds.** The format is fast and the thumb is twitchy. Open with motion, a face, a question, or a visual surprise — never with a slow logo reveal.

## 2. Sound-ON is the default — design audio first

Unlike most social platforms where sound-off is the safe assumption, Snapchat users overwhelmingly watch with sound on. This flips the creative brief:

- **Audio is a primary design element, not a post-production afterthought.** Write the voiceover, choose the music, and plan sound effects during concepting.
- **Use real human voices.** Voiceover from an actual person (not a synthetic read, not silent captions) fits the platform's conversational texture.
- **Music sets pace.** A track matched to the edit's energy keeps attention through the swipe-up moment. Mismatched or generic music is a skip trigger.
- **Effects punctuate.** Small sound cues — whooshes, pops, chimes — can carry transitions and emphasize the call to action.

Test your creative with the screen off: if the audio alone does not communicate the offer, it is under-designed.

## 3. AR Lenses: the platform's signature format

Lenses are Snapchat's distinctive high-engagement surface — interactive, branded augmented-reality experiences built in Lens Studio. The guidance positions them as the format where Snapchat most differs from every other ad platform:

- **Try-ons convert attention into intent.** Virtual product try-on (eyewear, cosmetics, apparel, accessories) lets users experience the product on their own face or body — a fundamentally stronger signal than a view.
- **Playable branded experiences.** Games, quizzes, and interactive filters built around the brand give users a reason to spend minutes, not seconds, with your creative.
- **Shareability is the distribution engine.** A good Lens gets sent to friends, which is earned reach no auction can buy. Design for the share moment: the funny transformation, the surprising result, the before-and-after.

Lenses cost more to produce than Snap Ads, so treat them as hero investments: one strong Lens per campaign beats three mediocre ones. Pair the Lens launch with supporting Snap Ads that drive users to try it.

## 4. Know the format lineup

Snapchat's official guidance describes a format for every job:

- **Snap Ads** — the core full-screen vertical video unit, skippable, with an attached swipe-up. Your everyday workhorse for traffic and conversion.
- **Story Ads** — a branded tile in the Discover feed that opens into a multi-snap story. Good for narrative: a 3–5 snap arc that builds a case before the ask.
- **Collection Ads** — the mini storefront: a hero creative on top plus tappable product tiles below. Purpose-built for catalogs and e-commerce — let shoppers browse without leaving the app.
- **Commercials** — longer-form video placements for brand storytelling and launches, where you need more than a few seconds.

Match the format to the job: Snap Ads for direct response, Story Ads for storytelling, Collection Ads for product browsing, Commercials for brand moments. Do not force a brand film into a Snap Ad slot.

## 5. Swipe-up is the conversion mechanism — reinforce it three ways

The swipe-up gesture is where attention becomes action, and the guidance is explicit that it needs reinforcement:

- **Verbal** — say it out loud in the voiceover: "swipe up to shop."
- **Visual** — show the gesture: an animated arrow, a hand graphic, the product moving upward.
- **Text** — put the instruction on screen in the final frames, paired with the offer.

All three, every time. A single subtle CTA at the end of a Snapchat ad is a conversion leak.

## 6. Clear the Gen Z authenticity bar

Snapchat's audience skews young, and the official creative guidance is blunt about what that means: overproduced, corporate-feeling creative underperforms. The aesthetic that wins:

- **Lo-fi and phone-filmed.** Creative that looks like it was shot on a phone by a real person outperforms studio polish. Slight imperfections are a feature.
- **Real people, not stock models.** Faces, friends, reactions, everyday settings. The feed is personal; ads that feel like messages from a friend win.
- **Native behavior.** Film like a Snap — vertical, close, candid, with on-screen text and stickers used the way actual users use them.

This is not permission to be sloppy; it is a direction to be deliberate about looking native. Every "rough" element should be a choice, not an accident.

## 7. Creative fatigue is fast — plan the refresh cycle

Snapchat creative wears out faster than on most platforms. The audience is young, the format is immersive, and repetition registers quickly:

- **Refresh creative every 1–2 weeks** for always-on campaigns. Treat this as a production calendar, not an emergency response.
- **Test 3–5 variants per concept.** Same offer, different hooks, different faces, different first two seconds. The hook is the variable that matters most.
- **Kill losers fast.** With rapid fatigue, a creative that is flat after a few days of delivery will not recover. Rotate in the next variant instead of nursing it.
- **Bank evergreen concepts.** Build a library of proven hooks and formats so refreshes are variations, not reinventions.

## 8. Frequency caps and dayparting

Two delivery disciplines protect both brand and budget:

- **Frequency caps to avoid annoyance.** In an immersive full-screen environment, overexposure feels invasive rather than merely repetitive. Cap impressions per user per day and watch for the point where additional frequency stops producing results and starts producing skips.
- **Daypart around usage peaks.** Snapchat usage concentrates in the **evening and late-night hours**. Schedule delivery — and time new creative launches — to align with those peaks rather than spreading budget evenly across dead hours.

Check performance by hour of day after the first week; the platform's aggregate pattern may differ from your audience's specific rhythm.

## 9. Measurement: the Snap Pixel

Install the Snap Pixel on the website and define the full conversion event set — views, adds to cart, purchases, signups. The guidance treats pixel data as the fuel for everything downstream:

- Conversion events feed the optimization system; without them, the algorithm cannot learn who converts.
- Build retargeting audiences from pixel events (site visitors, cart abandoners) — these are your highest-intent Snapchat audiences.
- For app campaigns, integrate the mobile measurement partner so install and post-install events flow back.

Verify the pixel fires correctly before spending — a broken event setup means the first week of budget teaches the system nothing.

## 10. The launch checklist

1. All creative shot or designed natively in 9:16, edge to edge, with a two-second hook.
2. Audio designed as a primary element: human voiceover, matched music, effects; passes the screen-off test.
3. One hero Lens (via Lens Studio) planned for the campaign's peak moment, supported by Snap Ads driving trial.
4. Format matched to objective: Snap Ads (response), Story Ads (narrative), Collection Ads (catalog), Commercials (brand).
5. Swipe-up reinforced verbally, visually, and in on-screen text on every conversion creative.
6. Creative passes the authenticity bar: lo-fi, real people, native Snap behavior.
7. Production calendar set: refresh every 1–2 weeks, 3–5 variants per concept in the bank.
8. Frequency caps applied; dayparting aligned to evening/late-night peaks.
9. Snap Pixel installed and verified on all key events.
10. Two-week learning window before judging performance.
