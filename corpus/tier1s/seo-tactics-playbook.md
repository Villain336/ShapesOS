# SEO Tactics Playbook

*Original synthesis of knowledge learned from the Ahrefs blog and Academy's practitioner content. Marketr-original words; factual attribution to Ahrefs.*

## Keyword research: hunt demand, not words

1. **Seed from the customer, expand with data.** Start with the phrases your buyers actually say (sales calls, support tickets, reviews), then expand with keyword tools: search volume, keyword difficulty, and — most importantly — the intent behind each term.
2. **Read the SERP before you write a word.** Google shows you what it wants: if the top 10 results for your target keyword are all listicles, your ultimate guide won't rank no matter how good it is. Match the dominant content type and angle, then beat it on depth, freshness, or originality.
3. **Keyword difficulty is a filter, not a verdict.** High-difficulty terms need authority you may not have; low-difficulty terms with clear intent and business value are where new sites win. New sites should live in the low-competition, high-intent quadrant until authority compounds.
4. **Parent topics over keywords.** Many keywords share one search intent and one ranking page. Group keywords by the page they'd share (the "parent topic") instead of writing a page per keyword — one strong page beats ten thin ones.

## Content that ranks: the checklist

- **Search intent match first.** Informational, navigational, commercial, transactional — identify the intent from the SERP and satisfy it completely in the first screen. Intent mismatch is the #1 reason good content doesn't rank.
- **Comprehensiveness with structure.** Cover the topic fully, but organize ruthlessly: descriptive H2/H3s, short paragraphs, tables for comparisons, visuals where they clarify. Walls of text don't get read; structured depth gets bookmarked and linked.
- **Originality is the ranking moat.** First-party data, original images and diagrams, expert quotes, unique frameworks. Content that says what everyone says earns what everyone earns: nothing.
- **Freshness as a feature.** For queries where recency matters (tools, statistics, "best X"), update the content genuinely — new data, new screenshots, new examples — and show the updated date. Date-swapping without substance is a trust killer when discovered.
- **Internal linking with intent.** Link new pages from relevant high-authority pages on your site with descriptive anchor text. Orphaned pages — even great ones — underperform because crawlers and users can't find them.

## Link building: earn, don't scheme

- **The durable tactics:** digital PR (newsworthy data and stories journalists want), original research and statistics (the most-linked content format on the internet), free tools and calculators (link magnets that keep earning), and broken-link building (find dead resources in your niche, offer yours as the replacement).
- **Content formats that attract links:** definitive guides, original studies, free tools, infographics with embed codes, and "ego bait" done right (expert roundups where contributors genuinely add value).
- **What to avoid:** paid links that pass PageRank, link farms, excessive exact-match anchor text, and reciprocal schemes. Link schemes are the fastest way to turn an asset into a liability — algorithm updates specifically hunt them.
- **Measure link quality, not count.** One link from a relevant, authoritative page in your niche outweighs fifty directory links. Relevance × authority × traffic is the mental model.

## Technical SEO: the table stakes

1. **Crawlability:** clean site architecture (every important page within 3 clicks of home), XML sitemaps submitted, robots.txt that doesn't accidentally block the good stuff, no orphan pages.
2. **Index control:** canonical tags on duplicates, noindex on thin/utility pages (tag archives, internal search results), parameter handling for faceted navigation.
3. **Core Web Vitals:** LCP, INP, CLS in the green — speed is a ranking factor and a conversion factor. Optimize images, defer non-critical JS, and fix layout shift.
4. **Structured data:** Article, Product, FAQ, HowTo, BreadcrumbList — marked up accurately and validated. Rich results earn the click even when you can't earn position one.
5. **Site migrations:** the highest-risk SEO event. Map every URL 1:1 with 301s, keep the information architecture stable, monitor indexation daily for a month. Most migration disasters are redirect disasters.

## The content audit cycle

Quarterly, grade every indexed page: **keep** (ranking or earning links — refresh lightly), **improve** (decaying rankings or thin vs. competitors — rewrite and relaunch), **consolidate** (multiple weak pages on one topic — merge into the strongest URL with redirects), **remove** (no traffic, no links, no value — 410 or redirect). Sites that prune dead content systematically outperform sites that publish endlessly — crawl budget and topical authority are finite.

## Measuring SEO honestly

Track **rankings by intent segment** (not just head terms), **organic traffic by page type**, **share of voice vs. competitors**, and **revenue/assisted revenue from organic** — never just "total organic traffic," which mixes brand and non-brand into a meaningless number. And remember the attribution lag: SEO investments made this quarter pay out over the next four. Judge SEO on trends and cohorts, not weeks.
