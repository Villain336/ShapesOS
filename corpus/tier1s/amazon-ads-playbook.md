# Amazon Ads for Marketers: A Retail-Media Playbook

*Original synthesis of knowledge learned from Amazon Ads Learning Console. Ideas are expressed in Marketr's own words; factual attribution to Amazon Ads Learning Console's official curriculum/guidance.*

## 1. Think shopping-intent media, not interruption media

Amazon is a store, so its ad inventory sits on top of shoppers who are already mid-decision. The core strategic insight: **relevance beats interruption.** On a social feed, the advertiser pays to borrow attention; on Amazon, the advertiser pays to be the most relevant answer to a question the shopper already asked. Everything downstream — keyword choices, creative, budgets — follows from that.

Practical consequence: the highest-leverage work is matching products to searches with genuine buying intent, not clever messaging. Write for the query, not for the audience persona.

## 2. Fix the listing before spending a dollar

The product detail page **is** the landing page. Ads can't outrun a weak listing. Before launching any campaign, audit each advertised product:

- **Star rating and review count.** Products under ~4 stars or with thin review counts convert poorly no matter how well targeted the traffic is. Fix product quality or review velocity first.
- **Price competitiveness.** Shoppers compare in one glance. If the price is materially above comparable listings, ad spend just funds other people's carts.
- **Prime eligibility and fulfillment.** Prime badges and fast delivery are conversion infrastructure. Non-Prime listings fighting Prime listings on the same keyword are at a structural disadvantage.
- **Images and bullets.** The ad only buys the click; the listing closes it. Lead image clarity, benefit-led bullets, and complete attributes (size, variant, use case) are prerequisites, not polish.

Rule: no product goes into paid promotion until its detail page would win a side-by-side comparison against the top three organic competitors on that keyword.

## 3. Sponsored Products: win the buy-box position on the right searches

Sponsored Products are keyword- and product-targeted placements that appear inside search results and on product detail pages. They are the workhorse of Amazon advertising.

- **Keyword targeting:** bid on exact, phrase, and broad matches. Start with the product's core terms (what it is), then expand to use-case terms (what it's for).
- **Product-attribute / ASIN targeting:** place your product on competitor detail pages and category pages. This is conquesting — intercepting shoppers who are looking at a rival.
- **Structure campaigns by intent, not by habit:** separate branded (your brand terms), category (generic product terms), and competitor campaigns. They have different economics and different success criteria; mixing them in one campaign hides all of that.
- **Default-bid discipline:** set campaign defaults conservatively, then adjust by match type and placement. The most common failure mode is one big campaign, one bid, no segmentation.

## 4. Sponsored Brands: tell the brand story at the top of results

Sponsored Brands put the brand above the fold on search — a headline, logo, and a curated set of products, or a brand video. Use them where storytelling matters:

- **Headline search ads** for brand terms and category terms where you want to own the top of the page with multiple products instead of fighting product-by-product.
- **Brand video** (the video format) for products where seeing it in use changes the decision — it auto-plays in results and demonstrates what static images can't.
- **Destination pages:** send brand traffic to a Store page or curated landing experience, not a single ASIN, so the brand halo lifts the whole catalog.

## 5. Sponsored Display and Amazon DSP: audience-based reach on and off Amazon

- **Sponsored Display** uses audience and views-based targeting — remarketing to shoppers who viewed your products but didn't buy, plus interest and category audiences, both on Amazon and across the wider web.
- **Amazon DSP (demand-side platform)** is the programmatic layer: reach audiences built from shopping signals across Amazon-owned properties and third-party inventory, with **new-to-brand measurement** showing which buyers had never purchased from your brand before.
- Use these as the upper- and mid-funnel: Sponsored Products harvest demand, Display/DSP create and recapture it. Budget them as a percentage of the harvesting engine, not as independent line items, unless the brand is large enough to measure both separately.

## 6. Budget allocation: branded, category, competitor

Split spend into three buckets and manage each to its own target:

1. **Branded (defense):** bid on your own brand terms. This is cheap, high-converting, and defensive — if you don't bid, competitors will. Cap spend by watching branded share of voice, not by chasing volume.
2. **Category (growth):** generic product terms where the shopper hasn't chosen a brand. This is the main growth engine; allocate the largest share here and manage to return on ad spend targets.
3. **Competitor conquesting (offense):** bid on rival brand terms and target their detail pages. Expect lower conversion rates and higher costs per click. Judge it on new-to-brand share and long-term value, not immediate return — and don't let it cannibalize the category budget.

Review the split monthly. The most common drift is conquesting quietly eating the category budget because it's exciting to report.

## 7. Search term reports: the negative-keyword engine

Amazon's search term reports show the actual queries that triggered your ads — and they will surprise you. Run this loop:

1. Pull the report weekly for active campaigns.
2. **Harvest:** queries that convert well and aren't yet exact-match keywords become new exact-match targets in a dedicated campaign.
3. **Exclude:** queries that spend without converting get added as **negative keywords** (block them at campaign or ad-group level).
4. **Exclude placements:** ASINs that take impressions without sales get added as **negative ASINs** so your product stops appearing on pages that never convert.

This loop is the single biggest lever on wasted spend. A campaign without regular negative-keyword hygiene will steadily drift toward irrelevant traffic.

## 8. New-to-brand metrics: separate growth from harvesting

Standard return metrics can't tell you whether an ad created a new customer or just rerouted an existing one. **New-to-brand metrics** (orders, sales, and customer counts from shoppers who hadn't bought from the brand in the lookback window) answer that directly.

- Judge Sponsored Brands, Display, and DSP primarily on new-to-brand share — that's their job.
- Judge Sponsored Products on blended return but watch the new-to-brand ratio; a campaign with great return and zero new-to-brand is harvesting, which is fine only if harvest is the goal.
- Set targets per bucket: e.g., category campaigns expected to deliver a minimum new-to-brand percentage, conquesting campaigns judged almost entirely on it.

## 9. The operating rhythm

Weekly: pull search term reports, harvest winners, add negatives, adjust bids on the 20% of keywords driving 80% of spend. Monthly: rebalance the branded/category/competitor split, audit listing health on advertised ASINs (ratings, price, stock), review new-to-brand trends per bucket. Quarterly: refresh Sponsored Brands creative and Store pages, test one new audience or placement type, benchmark category share of voice. Continuous: out-of-stock products must be paused from advertising the same day — paying for clicks on an unavailable product is pure waste.
