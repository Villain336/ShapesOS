# Inbound Marketing Playbook

*Original synthesis of knowledge learned from HubSpot Academy. Marketr-original words; factual attribution to HubSpot's official curriculum.*

## The inbound doctrine in one paragraph

Stop interrupting people who don't want you and start attracting people who need you. Inbound marketing earns attention by publishing genuinely useful content, converts that attention with low-friction offers, nurtures the resulting contacts with relevant follow-up, and turns customers into promoters. Every tactic below is a gear in that machine — a gear that spins alone is just content.

## The flywheel: attract, engage, delight

Think in a flywheel, not a funnel. A funnel ends at the customer; a flywheel feeds on them — delighted customers create referrals, reviews, and word-of-mouth that attract the next cycle, and momentum compounds.

1. **Attract** — the right strangers, with content that answers the questions they're already asking. Blog posts, guides, videos, and tools built around real search intent and real buyer questions.
2. **Engage** — turn visitors into contacts and contacts into customers. Lead magnets, conversational touchpoints, and sales enablement that respects where the buyer is.
3. **Delight** — onboarding, support content, and proactive check-ins that make customers successful. Success stories become attract-stage content. The wheel turns.

Friction anywhere slows the wheel; force (discounts, pressure tactics) spins it fast and breaks it faster.

## Content that attracts: the topic-cluster model

- **Pillar pages + topic clusters.** One comprehensive pillar page per core topic, surrounded by cluster content (blog posts, guides) that each target a specific long-tail question and link back to the pillar. This architecture does double duty: it organizes the site for humans and concentrates topical authority for search engines.
- **Write for the question behind the keyword.** "CRM pricing" isn't a keyword — it's a buyer asking "can I afford this and is it worth it?" Answer the question directly, completely, and honestly (including when the honest answer is "it depends").
- **The 80/20 of formats:** how-to guides and templates (highest compounding traffic), original research and data (highest link-earning), tools and calculators (highest engagement), and customer stories (highest conversion assist). Pick two and do them well rather than five poorly.

## Conversion paths: from stranger to contact

Every piece of attract-stage content needs a next step:

1. **The offer must match the intent.** A visitor reading "how to choose a CRM" is not ready for a demo — offer the comparison template. A visitor on the pricing page is — offer the trial. Mismatched offers are the #1 conversion-path failure.
2. **Landing pages with one job.** One offer, one form, one CTA. Navigation stripped, copy focused on the outcome, form fields minimized to what you'll actually use. Every extra field costs conversions; every extra link costs focus.
3. **Progressive profiling.** Don't ask for everything on first touch — collect name and email, then enrich on subsequent conversions. The CRM should remember what it already knows.
4. **Thank-you pages are underused real estate.** Confirm, set expectations ("here's what happens next"), and offer the logical next step — never a dead end.

## Lead nurturing: the middle that most teams skip

Most leads aren't ready to buy — nurturing is how you stay present until they are:

- **Segment by behavior and lifecycle stage**, not just demographics. A lead who downloaded the pricing guide twice is not the same as one who read a top-of-funnel blog post.
- **Workflows with exit conditions.** Every automated sequence needs a goal (the action that ends the sequence) and a suppression list (who should never enter). Sequences without exits become spam with extra steps.
- **Lead scoring that sales trusts.** Explicit scoring (fit: title, company size, industry) plus implicit scoring (behavior: pages viewed, content downloaded, emails engaged). Set the sales-ready threshold *with* sales, review it quarterly, and send sales the "why" behind every handoff — score alone is not context.
- **Sales and marketing SLA.** Marketing commits to lead volume and quality; sales commits to follow-up speed and feedback. Review the SLA monthly. The funnel has two owners or it has none.

## Delight: the compounding layer

- **Onboarding content** (welcome sequences, setup guides, quick-win checklists) determines whether a new customer becomes a retained one. The first 30 days are the whole game.
- **Knowledge base and self-service** deflect support tickets and double as SEO assets — every answered question is a page that can rank.
- **Feedback loops:** NPS/CSAT surveys, review requests at moments of delight (not randomly), and a closed loop where feedback visibly changes the product. Customers who feel heard become the flywheel's fuel: testimonials, case studies, referrals.

## Measuring inbound

The metrics that matter, in order: **organic traffic growth** (is attract working?), **visitor-to-lead rate** (are conversion paths working?), **lead-to-customer rate** (is nurture + sales working?), **CAC and CAC payback** (is the machine economical?), and **customer lifetime value** (is delight compounding?). Vanity metrics — pageviews, social followers, email opens — are diagnostic, never the headline.
